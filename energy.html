Forecasting demand and critical-load risk

Temperature-Driven Electricity Demand Analysis

Overview

Weather is one of the strongest external drivers of electricity demand. This project builds an end-to-end analysis—from data preparation to modeling and scenario interpretation to support operational planning under cold-weather stress conditions.

Key outputs

  • Demand–temperature relationship quantified with interpretable models
  • Critical load risk estimation for extreme cold scenarios
  • Decision-ready visuals and thresholds for planning and communication

Solution

Problem 1 : Demand changes non-linearly with temperature

Electricity demand does not respond to temperature in a simple linear way, especially under extreme cold conditions. This makes planning difficult using averages alone.

💡 Solution: I modeled demand as a function of temperature with approaches that capture non-linear behavior and validated fit using out-of-sample checks.

Why this matters: improves forecast reliability when conditions deviate from “normal” weather.

Problem 2 : Stakeholders need risk, not just point forecasts

A single predicted demand value is hard to operationalize without understanding the likelihood of crossing critical thresholds.

💡 Solution: I estimated the probability of entering a critical-load regime and visualized risk across temperature scenarios.

Why this matters: supports early actions when risk is high even before demand peaks.

Problem 3 : Cold-weather stress needs scenario-based interpretation

Extreme events are rare but costly; teams need simple scenario narratives to understand what the models imply operationally.

💡 Solution: I ran cold-weather scenarios (e.g., very low mean temperature) and translated results into operational implications and decision thresholds.

Why this matters: makes the analysis actionable for planning and communication.

Customer data analysis

From Transactions to Actionable Customer Segments

Overview

I built a customer segmentation framework from 3 years of transaction data for a digital banking app. The goal was to turn raw transaction logs into actionable customer segments that product and marketing teams can use for personalization and CLV growth. The pipeline: feature engineering → preprocessing → PCA → K-means++ → evaluation → interpretation.

What I delivered:

  • 5 behavioral segments based on customer-level RFM+ features
  • Stakeholder-ready segment definitions (plain-language summaries + visuals)
  • Action guidance (what to do with each segment: retention / upsell / reactivation)

My role

I owned the analytical core of this project : from feature design and clustering decisions to segment interpretation, ensuring the output was both statistically sound and usable for business teams.

  • Feature engineering: designed customer-level aggregation
  • Preprocessing: stabilized skewed transaction variables via log transforms + scaling for distance-based clustering
  • PCA: reduced redundancy/multicollinearity to improve cluster separability and interpretability
  • Clustering: implemented K-means++ and selected K using Elbow + Silhouette trade-offs
  • Interpretation: created segment definitions and analytical visuals to support stakeholder understanding
Team contributions
  • Me: analytics pipeline, modeling, evaluation, interpretation, visualization
  • Teammates: persona creation, visual design, business recommendations, coordination

Target users

Product, marketing, and growth teams at a digital banking app

Timeline

5 weeks

What is the business impact?

  • Personalization foundation: replaced “average user” assumptions with segment-level behavior patterns
  • Growth levers: clarified which users to retain, which to upsell, and which to reactivate
  • Operational usability: delivered interpretable segments and visuals that non-technical teams can act on

Solution

Problem 1 : Customers were treated as one “average user”

User behavior differed significantly across customers, but strategy lacked a clear segmentation model. This limited targeted engagement and CLV growth.

💡 Solution: I engineered customer-level RFM+ behavioral features and segmented users with PCA + K-means++ into five distinct clusters.

Problem 2 : Statistically valid clusters were hard to interpret

Clusters can be mathematically “good” but still fail if stakeholders cannot understand what each segment represents.

💡 Solution: I characterized clusters using spending scale, frequency, recency, and category patterns, then created clear segment summaries and visuals to make them decision-ready.

Problem 3 : Insights didn’t translate into clear actions

Even strong segmentation is wasted unless it informs concrete engagement strategies and prioritization.

💡 Solution: I supported an action mapping for each segment (retention / upsell / reactivation) so the segmentation can be operationalized in campaigns and product decisions.

Clustering Results

The clustering analysis revealed five distinct behavioral segments. To make the results usable for non-technical teams, each cluster was interpreted in terms of spending behavior, activity level, and real-world usage patterns.

[Cluster comparison charts]

The charts highlight clear differences in how each segment uses the product. Cluster 0 shows high transaction frequency with low average amounts, indicating frequent everyday usage. In contrast, Cluster 2 and Cluster 4 are characterized by lower frequency but significantly higher transaction values, reflecting more deliberate, goal-driven usage such as savings or occasional large payments.

Activity patterns further separate the segments. Cluster 3 stands out with long recency and minimal activity, signaling dormant or churned users, while Cluster 0 and Cluster 1 remain consistently active with shorter time since last use. These differences suggest distinct engagement and churn risk profiles across segments.

Taken together, the visualizations show that customer value is driven by different behavioral mechanisms—frequency for some segments and transaction size for others—underscoring the need for segment-specific engagement strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

Action guidance by segment

  • Frequent small spenders: focus on retention through convenience and reliability
  • Light but active users: encourage upsell by highlighting additional features and value
  • Occasional high spenders: support high-value moments with tailored offers
  • Dormant users: prioritize reactivation with targeted reminders and incentives
  • Savings-focused users: reinforce trust and simplify large-value transactions

Clustering Process

1. Overview

Goal: Identify customer segments based on transaction behavior
Dataset: 3 years of Y banking transaction data
Methods: Feature engineering, PCA, K-means clustering
Outcome: 5 distinct customer segments with business insights

2. Data preperation & preprocessing

Original fields: customer_id, country, type, trx_date, amount_chf, orig_currency, category, mcc, counterpart
Output: aggregated customer table with engineered features

  • Frequency: n_tx, median_tx_per_month
  • Scale: total_spend, spend_per_month, median_amount
  • Variability: std_dev_amount, pct_high_value_tx
  • Recency: recency_days
[The original dataset and new variables]

3. Preprocessing

Transaction distributions were skewed and heavy-tailed, which can distort distance-based clustering. I applied transformations to stabilize the feature space.

  • Log transform: reduces skew and sensitivity to extreme values
  • Scaling: aligns feature ranges to avoid dominance by high-magnitude variables
[Transformations applied to stabilize clustering]

4. Dimensionality reduction (PCA)

Many engineered features were correlated. I used PCA to reduce multicollinearity and noise while preserving the behavioral signal.

  • Why PCA: improves separability and robustness for distance-based clustering
  • Result: retained 5 components (~95% variance) for clustering

5. Clustering & selecting K

I applied K-means++ and selected the number of clusters by balancing quantitative metrics with business interpretability.

  • Algorithm: K-means++
  • Model selection: Elbow method + Silhouette score
  • Why K=5: best trade-off between separation and stakeholder-usable segment granularity
